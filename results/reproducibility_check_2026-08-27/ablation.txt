Reading this table

   Only the final calibration is within 1 % of the reference. The
   A4-based calibration, which covers 7.3 % of the frame, is the worst
   despite having the highest perspective variation: a small reference
   patch extrapolates badly. Perspective variation alone is not
   sufficient; it must come with coverage.

   The inherited pixel threshold reaches the WRONG verdict on this
   pair: 667 px against a 80 px threshold reads as compliant, while the
   true separation of 1.48 m is below the 2.0 m limit. A threshold expressed
   in pixels encodes the geometry of the camera it was tuned on.

Two limits on what this shows

   1. The reference lies at near-constant image depth (y = 224 and 221),
      so perspective correction barely affects it. This validates
      absolute scale, not perspective correction, which is why even the
      degenerate calibration scores reasonably here.
   2. The pixel threshold was tuned for the baseline author's camera.
      This shows such a threshold does not transfer between
      installations, not that the baseline was wrong in its own setting.
